Contemporary Indian wear on the streets of Prague

5 August 2026 · 6 min read

When people in Prague look for Indian fashion, they are pushed toward one narrow aesthetic — either the hippie movement of Goa or elaborate traditional wear like the sari or kurta. These hold their own cultural place, but they are increasingly dated, and they do not represent India's diverse and rapidly evolving fashion landscape.

Living in Prague and sharing my daily wardrobe choices, I frequently get questions from locals about where my outfits come from. Some even take my photos. The pieces that draw the most curiosity are not the glittering festival wear, but modern, structured garments made from breathable, hand-loomed fabrics.

In India, a significant fashion shift is under way. Designers are moving away from excess, focusing instead on minimalist silhouettes, sustainable practices, and heritage textiles adapted for global, everyday wear — bringing local artisans to the global fashion front and transforming traditional Indian clothes into contemporary silhouettes. For those in Czechia looking to diversify their wardrobe with unique, high-quality pieces, here is an introduction to the new age of Indian wear, and how it fits into the European aesthetic.

Finding these pieces in Prague

Through my platform, Indian in Prague, I regularly share curated pre-loved finds on Vinted — showing how high-quality, design-led contemporary Indian pieces can find a second life in European closets. By choosing pre-owned or carefully sourced garments, we move away from mass-produced trends and toward a more conscious, globally inspired personal style.

Here are the core contemporary movements changing how Indian fashion is viewed and loved abroad.

01The modern Indian aesthetic

Indian fashion commentary highlights a distinct transition toward earthy palettes, organic textures and functional design. In contrast to the stereotype of neon pinks and metallic golds, modern Indian everyday wear often embraces a subdued, sophisticated palette. Brands are pioneering a style that blends tropical ease with Scandinavian minimalism: simple linen shirt-dresses, ivory overlay jackets, and wide-leg trousers in olive, slate, terracotta and charcoal.

Fitting PragueThese pieces integrate into a capsule wardrobe. An ivory Chanderi silk-cotton tunic layered over fitted trousers or denim matches Prague's understated, elegant street style — and stays comfortable through tram commutes and café afternoons.

02Contemporary craft and conscious living

Sustainability sits at the core of the new Indian wear movement. Rather than mass-produced fast fashion, contemporary brands focus on slow, ethical production that supports rural artisans. They use hand-embroidered mirror work and appliqué on simple cotton dresses, keeping traditional crafts alive in modern cuts. Many are becoming renowned for preserving hand-block printing and using natural dyes to create intricate geometric and floral patterns on lightweight cotton.

Fitting PragueFor the eco-conscious Czech demographic that values durability and craftsmanship, these brands offer an alternative to high-street labels. A block-printed dress with a denim jacket and leather boots bridges the gap between traditional Indian craft and European autumn styling.

03Curation and avant-garde fusion

For those seeking unique statement pieces, curated platforms aggregate a range of boutiques and design houses, offering a collective look at Indian fashion. On these platforms, fusion wear is reimagining traditional garments: longline capes printed with subtle Indian motifs like Ajrakh or Dabu, structured silk jackets, and asymmetrical tunics.

Fitting PragueThese garments function as wearable art. A structured, hand-woven jacket styled over a black turtleneck and tailored trousers gives a sophisticated layer for Prague's cooler seasons.

How to style new-age Indian wear in Central Europe

Bringing these pieces into a European wardrobe does not require a complete style overhaul. It is about layering, balancing proportions, and mixing textures.

  • The layered tunic: a long cotton-silk tunic (historically worn as a kurta) works open as a light duster over a basic tee and jeans in spring.
  • Textural contrast: pair a heavily textured, hand-spun cotton shirt with structured trousers or a skirt — raw, organic fabric against polished finishes.
  • Accessorising simply yet boldly: instead of heavy jewellery, Indian styling leans on subtle-but-striking accents — several plain brass cuffs layered together, a hand-woven linen scarf, or a pair of flats (juttis).

Indian restaurants

Annamalai

Tastes like home

4 August 2026 · Nové Město · South Indian · sit-down

Everything about Annamalai sets it apart from other South Indian restaurants in the city. They prepare dishes on the spot when you order (it takes time to arrive and is worth the wait). You can tell it is home cooked, tastes vary from day to day, as they are supposed to when being prepared fresh everytime, but everything remains tasty. Super light on the tummy, and you can vary the spice level by asking them. In any other place I would mind the TV blaring Indian songs, but somehow, the vibe of the place is so authentic, with underground, dim-lit room, small and cosy, adorned with old school decor, that the TV with songs back to back actually reminds me of local joints back home. I love the peace, you can spend good time without being disturbed. On weekends they do special combo meals like Chicken Biryani with Chicken 65, boiled egg and yoghurt (although I am vegetarian, my partner tried this and loved it). Staff is relaxed and respectful, and one of them is a freelance student of Charles University and is very attentive and polite. The only South Indian I keep going back to.

Order: dosa, idli, and medu vada.

INDelicious Bistro

Street food

3 August 2026 · Nusle · North/South Indian · quick bites and full meals

Another Indian restaurant I keep going back to. It boils down to taste and despite being super tasty, I never feel bloated or super full after the meal. Really grateful for street food from across India.

Order: pav bhaji, spinach paneer paratha, vada pav, bhel.

Vegan in Prague

Creative plant-based food. Indian food is a cheat code for vegans — whole cuisines built on lentils, chickpeas and vegetables, no imitation anything. These are the places (Indian and not) I go back to.

Dosa Dosa

Fully vegan

2 August 2026 · Újezd · South Indian · fully vegan

Again, a repeat favourite and a perfect breakfast stop. I love the California dosa, masala dosa, uttapam and buckwheat noodles. Cold brew and desserts not to be missed.

Order: California dosa, masala dosa, uttapam.

Pastva

Fully vegan

1 August 2026 · Anděl · European · fully vegan

They rotate their menu, friendly staff, ridiculously tasty dishes. Love that they try to serve new cuisines. Burgers, risotto, dumplings, borscht, hummus, croquettes are my favourite here.

Order: whatever is on the rotating menu.

Palo Verde Bistro

Fully vegan

30 July 2026 · Žitná · European · fully vegan

Dreamy decor, love the mushroom lamps, and the cosmic skies! Super tasty spinach chimney and parmesan burger.

Order: spinach chimney, parmesan burger.
