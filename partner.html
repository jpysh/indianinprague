Why now

Three rules changed. Two shut the door, one opens it.

€3 on every parcel — since July 2026

The EU’s customs reform put a per-item charge on low-value imports and ended the cheap-parcel model outright. A single posted parcel from India no longer competes on price.

Transitional until the EU Data Hub in 2028, with an import handling fee due by November 2026.

No EU person, no listing — since December 2024

The General Product Safety Regulation bars a non-EU seller without an EU-established Responsible Person. In June 2026 German and French authorities ran a joint sweep of Amazon listings missing compliance data and issued 14-day removal notices.

The single hardest requirement for a small Indian maker to satisfy alone, and the one Guldaar exists to absorb.

Under the India–EU trade agreement, textiles, jewellery, handicrafts and leather go to zero duty, with provisional application expected from late 2026.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Who is legally responsible for my products in Europe?

Guldaar is. The General Product Safety Regulation requires an EU-established Responsible Person for every consumer product sold to EU consumers — named on the listing, reachable by an authority, holding a technical file for ten years. Guldaar holds that role for everything on the rail and is the importer of record on EU-held stock.

Under the 2026 EU customs reform Guldaar is also the deemed importer on goods shipped directly from the atelier, and as a marketplace operator it keeps trader-traceability records under Article 30 of the Digital Services Act.

Do I have to give up my stock or my other channels?

No. The contract is semi-managed: you own the goods the whole way, and Guldaar owns logistics, compliance, listing and demand. Keep selling wherever you already sell.

Commission on marketplace sales is zero, because the marketplace has already taken 8–15% and stacking a second cut is the model this was built against.

How does the money get back to India?

You export under your own IEC and LUT, so the export stays yours: the RoDTEP scrip is yours to claim, and the e-BRC is self-certified against the inward remittance shown on your own bank’s statement, as the DGFT framework provides for.

Guldaar buys FOB India and becomes the importer of record in Spain. Nothing sits in an intermediary account waiting to be reconciled.

What does it cost, exactly?

€29 a month at the founding rate, €79 at list. 0% commission on marketplace sales, 5% on sales made on guldaarbazaar.com, and logistics billed at cost + 10% with the underlying cost visible to you.

The own-store rate has a published path to 10%, and the condition is stated rather than reserved: it moves only once own-store sales sustain €25,000 a month for three consecutive months, and merchants who joined before that keep 5% for a further twelve months.

What happens if a product fails a compliance check?

It does not get listed. REACH certification is a hard gate before listing for jewellery and metalware, compliance is filed per product family rather than per item, and no family enters the catalogue without a file.

If a test fails we tell you what failed and what would fix it. What we will not do is list it anyway and hope, because the name on the listing is ours.

How fast does delivery actually reach a customer?

Two tiers. Bestsellers held as EU stock in Spain reach a Spanish address in 1–2 days and Germany, France or Italy in 2–4 days. Everything else ships from the atelier in 8–14 days, duty and VAT paid at checkout so nothing is demanded at the door.

A product earns its way from the second tier into the first on its own sales, reviewed weekly, and nothing holds warehouse space beyond eight weeks of demonstrated sell-through.

Who owns the customer relationship?

Guldaar carries the demand, the payment and the returns, so the customer transacts with Guldaar. You get your own sales visible to you monthly, per product and per channel, including the per-order cost that produced them.

If a channel loses money for you, you will see it in that statement before we do anything about it.

Why should I believe any of this?

Because the operator is the first merchant on it. Guldaar Bazaar sells its own goods into Europe through the same rail, the same compliance files and the same delivery promise, and the numbers on this page are the ones it runs on rather than a projection of someone else’s.

Ask for the parts that are not public. A serious question gets a specific answer, including the ones where the answer is that something is still being built.
